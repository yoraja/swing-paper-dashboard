Backtest results (2016–2025)

  • Universe: point-in-time Nasdaq-100. A stock trades only while it is an index member, which removes survivorship bias. About 34 former members that have no price history available are missing.
  • Data: daily Yahoo prices, real prices adjusted for splits only (not for dividends), the same prices a default chart shows. Results were rebuilt on these prices on Nov 2, 2026; the earlier version used dividend-adjusted prices.
  • Costs: 5 bps slippage per side, $0.005 per share, and 10 bps extra on stop fills.
  • Sizing: $100k start, 2% risk per trade, at most 10 positions. Only setups with at least 20 trades are shown. Win rate = share of trades that made money.
  • Options: priced with a Black-Scholes model (implied volatility estimated from realized volatility). These are not historical option fills.
  • Dividends: the backtest does not credit dividends, so compare it with the price-only benchmarks.

Takeaways: - No setup beat buy-and-hold over this period. The best monthly and quarterly setups made about 5–9% a year, with shallower drawdowns than the index. - With at least 30 trades, three long setups win more than half the time: Mother Bar Breakout on quarterly bars (70%), Continuation Divergence on monthly bars (60%) and Shakeout Breakout on monthly bars (54%). - Mother Bar Breakout on monthly bars dropped to a 46% win rate on real prices (54% on dividend-adjusted prices), though it still made about 6% a year. - Daily-timeframe versions made 0–5% after costs. Every short version lost money. - Modeled at-the-money calls improved some monthly results, but deepened drawdowns.
